ABOUT INAZUMA

About Inazuma, known as the Nation of Eternity, is a captivating archipelago where striking violet foliage meets tranquil coastal waters. Renowned for its time-honored traditions, sacred mountain shrines, and historic castle architecture, it offers travelers a serene yet culturally rich island escape. From bustling port streets to quiet cliffside sanctuaries, every island in Inazuma preserves centuries of proud heritage and natural beauty.

You’ll enjoy this trip if...

You love picturesque islands adorned with cherry blossoms, tranquil gardens, and breathtaking coastal views.

You are fascinated by samurai culture, legendary swordsmen,and stories of honor passed down through generations.

You enjoy visiting sacred shrines, ancient castles, and spiritual landmarks steeped in tradition and mystery.

You like trying unique regional delicacies, from sweet dango treats to fresh seafood caught along the islands.

You crave adventure and are drawn to dramatic ocean scenery, rugged landscapes, and an atmosphere filled with legends.

YOUR STOPS

A closer look at the destinations waiting for you on this expedition.

NAKURAMI ISLAND GRAND SHRINE

Perched high atop Mt. Yougou, the Narukami Island Grand Shrine welcomes travelers with its iconic Sacred Sakura tree and winding path of crimson torii gates. Stroll through the tranquil courtyard, draw a traditional fortune slip, or take in sweeping panoramic views of the surrounding islands and open sea. The crisp mountain air and gentle rustle of violet blossoms create a peaceful atmosphere unlike anywhere else in Teyvat. Every step along this sacred summit offers a memorable glimpse into Inazuma's spiritual heart.

RITOU TRADING PORT

Serving as the vibrant maritime entrance to Inazuma, Ritou Trading Port blends busy merchant docks with picturesque autumn-hued maple pathways. Wander along the wooden waterfront piers, sample authentic local street snacks, or browse unique island crafts from international traders. As the afternoon sun reflects off the harbor waters, the port comes alive with warm lantern light and lively coastal charm. It is the ideal starting point for travelers eager to experience Inazuma's daily rhythm.

TENSHUKAKU

Rising proudly above the tiered rooftops of Inazuma City, Tenshukaku stands as a breathtaking masterpiece of traditional fortress architecture and royal heritage. Admire its sweeping curved eaves, intricate timber craftsmanship, and commanding stone walls from the surrounding scenic viewpoints. Visitors can walk the historic avenues leading up to the citadel while learning about the stories that shaped the archipelago. Standing before Tenshukaku offers an unforgettable sense of grandeur and timeless elegance.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What should I wear for the Inazuma tour? +

Modest, comfortable layered clothing and slip-on walking shoes with sturdy grip—ideal for shrine visits and climbing the stone steps of Mt. Yougou (sacred mountain).

What should I bring with me? +

Bring your INAZ-03 confirmation, maritime travel permit/ID, a compact umbrella for sudden coastal showers, and small Mora (local currency) denominations for shrine offerings and street snacks.

Is the tour suitable for families and children? +

Yes, families are welcome, though the climb through the Thousand Torii Gates (shrine archways) to the Grand Narukami Shrine involves sustained stairways that require steady pacing for young children.

How much walking is involved? +

Moderate to high walking (3 to 4 hours daily), primarily along coastal port walkways in Ritou and uphill stone steps leading to Narukami Shrine.

Can I explore on my own during the trip? +

Yes. You will have free time on Day 3 evening in Inazuma City’s Hanamizaka (artisan shopping street) district and Day 4 morning in Ritou before departure.

Are there any rules or protocols I should follow during the tour? +

Observe traditional shrine etiquette (bow at the torii archways and keep voices low at Grand Narukami Shrine), obey Tenryou Commission (local security force) perimeter rules around Tenshukaku, and keep your Ritou travel pass on hand at all times.
